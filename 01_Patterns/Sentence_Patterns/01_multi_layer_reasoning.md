# 01 – Multi-layer reasoning
**Pattern:** Huvudsats + bisats + bisats + bisats + att-sats + passiv bisats

```
■ HUVUDSATS ──▶ ▷ BISATS ──▶ ▷ BISATS ──▶ ▷ BISATS ──▶ ✦ ATT-SATS ──▶ ▣ PASSIV BISATS
■ main   ▷ subordinate   ◆ relative   ✦ that-clause   ▣ passive
```

🧠 **Remember it as "an onion of clauses":** peel one bisats layer at a time until you reach the core thought (att-sats + passive).

**English:** Main clause + subordinate clause + subordinate clause + subordinate clause + that-clause + passive subordinate clause

> **Läraren förklarade** *när lektionen började*, *eftersom eleverna inte förstod*, *att uppgiften* *skulle göras om*.
> *(The teacher explained, when the lesson started, since the students didn't understand, that the task would be redone.)*

## 🧠 Mental model — nested boxes, not a flat chain

**General principle first:** whenever a single idea has several layers of dependency — a claim that depends on a cause, which depends on a decision, which only matters for a specific outcome — the sentence nests those layers instead of listing them side by side. Each clause is only meaningful *inside* the one before it, the same way you can't understand step 3 of an argument without already knowing steps 1 and 2. That's true in any language, not just Swedish; multi-layer reasoning is simply what it looks like when a sentence's *shape* mirrors the *shape of the thought* behind it.

**Applied to the example above:** **Läraren förklarade** [ **när** lektionen började [ **eftersom** eleverna inte förstod [ **att** uppgiften [ skulle **göras om** ] ] ] ] — you can only "open" a bisats once the box surrounding it is already open, exactly like you can't evaluate a conclusion before you've been given its premises.

## ❓ Why does this pattern appear?

**In general**, multi-clause nesting shows up whenever a speaker needs to deliver several linked facts as *one* coherent thought, instead of as separate sentences the listener would have to mentally reconnect themselves. Three ordinary communicative needs — present in any language, any topic — are what force clauses to nest rather than stay separate:

1. **A claim often needs its own cause.** Simply stating that something happened is incomplete if the listener would immediately ask "why?" — so the reason gets folded into the same sentence as a subordinate clause, instead of being left for a follow-up question.
2. **A report needs its exact content spelled out.** Saying "she explained" or "he decided" is meaningless on its own — you always have to say *what* was explained or decided, which is exactly the job of an att-sats (that-clause) in Swedish (or "that…" in English).
3. **An outcome often matters more than who caused it.** When the actor is irrelevant or unknown, the reported content naturally shifts into passive voice — "the task would be redone" rather than "someone would redo the task" (see [04_passiv_s_passiv.md](../../02_Grammar/02_Verb/04_passiv_s_passiv.md)).

Put together, these three needs are why **teaching, policy-making, journalism, and academic writing** are the registers where this pattern appears constantly: all four require a speaker to sound thorough and justified, not just informative — and packing cause, decision, and outcome into a single sentence is precisely what signals "these facts are logically connected", rather than "these facts just happen to be next to each other."

> 💡 **The cognitive limit behind it:** human working memory can only hold a handful of "open loops" at once (a subject waiting for its verb, a bisats waiting to close). That's exactly why natural embedding rarely goes past 3–4 nested layers — this pattern already sits near that ceiling, which is why it reads as advanced (B2–C1) rather than beginner (A1–A2) Swedish.

Use of multi layer reasoning allows you to express complex ideas and relationships between different parts of a sentence. It can help you convey more information in a single sentence and create a more nuanced and detailed expression of your thoughts.

examples: Pattern: Huvudsats + relativsats + bisats (att) + bisats (när) + huvudsats + bisats (för att)  
- school rules:
> **Skolan har en regel** *som säger* *att eleverna måste vara tysta* *när läraren pratar* **för att skapa en bättre inlärningsmiljö**.
- Skolan har en regel (main clause) + som säger (relative clause) + att eleverna måste vara tysta (subordinate clause) + när läraren pratar (subordinate clause) + för att skapa en bättre inlärningsmiljö (purpose clause)  - School has a rule that says that students must be quiet when the teacher talks in order to create a better learning environment.
- school play:
> **Skolans teatergrupp** *som består av elever från olika årskurser* **uppträdde** *när skolan firade sin 100-årsdag* **för att underhålla eleverna och personalen**.
- Skolans teatergrupp (main clause) + som består av elever från olika årskurser (relative clause) + uppträdde (main clause) + när skolan firade sin 100-årsdag (subordinate clause) + för att underhålla eleverna och personalen (purpose clause) - The school's theater group, which consists of students from different grades, performed when the school celebrated its 100th anniversary in order to entertain the students and staff.
- pedagog 
> **Pedagogen** *som har lång erfarenhet av att arbeta med barn* **förklarade** *att det är viktigt att skapa en trygg och stimulerande miljö* *när barnen lär sig* **för att främja deras utveckling**.
- Pedagogen (main clause) + som har lång erfarenhet av att arbeta med barn (relative clause) + förklarade (main clause) + att det är viktigt att skapa en trygg och stimulerande miljö (subordinate clause) + när barnen lär sig (subordinate clause) + för att främja deras utveckling (purpose clause) - The educator, who has long experience working with children, explained that it is important to create a safe and stimulating environment when children learn in order to promote their development.

relative sats (som) possibilities
- som säger att (that says that)
- som består av (which consists of)
- som har (who has)
- som arbetar med (who works with)
- som är (which is)

is verb followed after som? Yes, the verb typically follows "som" in a relative clause. For example:
- Skolan har en regel **som säger** att eleverna måste vara tysta. (The school has a rule that says that students must be quiet.)
- Pedagogen **som har** lång erfarenhet av att arbeta med barn förklarade... (The educator who has long experience working with children explained...)
