# Konjunktioner: och, men, för, så

> 🧠 **Mental model — equal-rank couplers:** och/men/för/så always connect two huvudsatser of the same rank, like linking two train cars of equal size — word order never changes on either side of the coupler.

## 📜 Background — the oldest, simplest connective words

Coordinating two equal-rank clauses with a short connector ("and", "but") is one of the most ancient and stable syntactic patterns in human language — nearly every language family has a small, closed set of these words, and they change very slowly over time precisely because they're used so constantly. **Och** is directly cognate with English **and** (both from the same Proto-Germanic root), and **för** here is the same word as the preposition "for" used causally, exactly like English *"we stayed home, **for** it was raining"* — an older, more literary use of "for" that survives in Swedish as an everyday causal conjunction.

## Examples

- Jag äter frukost **och** dricker kaffe. (I eat breakfast and drink coffee.)
- Han är trött, **men** han jobbar vidare. (He is tired, but he keeps working.)
- Vi stannar hemma, **för** det regnar. (We stay home, for it's raining.)
- Hon pluggade mycket, **så** hon klarade provet. (She studied a lot, so she passed the test.)
