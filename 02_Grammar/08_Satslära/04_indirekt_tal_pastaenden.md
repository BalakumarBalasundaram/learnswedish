# Indirekt tal (påståenden)

> 🧠 **Mental model — the reported-news filter:** a direct quote ("Jag är trött", sa hon) passes through the "att" filter and comes out as a bisats ("Hon sa **att** hon var trött") — like a journalist paraphrasing a quote instead of repeating it word for word; the tense usually shifts one step back.
## 📜 Background — why the tense shifts backward

This backward tense-shift ("är" → "var") is called **sequence of tense**, and it's a widespread cross-linguistic pattern (English does it too: *"I am tired" → she said she **was** tired*). The logic is about distancing: once a statement is being *reported* rather than spoken live, the point of view shifts to the reporter's "now", which is later than the original speaker's "now" — so the tense moves one notch into the past to reflect that shift in perspective. The **att**-filter itself reuses the identical complementizer from [02_subjunktioner.md](02_subjunktioner.md) — the same all-purpose "downgrade ticket" word does double duty both for plain subordinate clauses and for wrapping a quoted statement.
## Examples

- Direkt: "Jag är trött", sa hon. → Indirekt: Hon sa **att** hon **var** trött. (She said that she was tired.)
- Direkt: "Vi kommer imorgon", sa de. → Indirekt: De sa **att** de **skulle komma** nästa dag. (They said that they would come the next day.)
