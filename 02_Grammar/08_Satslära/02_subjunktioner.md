# Subjunktioner: att, eftersom, om, när

> 🧠 **Mental model — the downgrade ticket:** att/eftersom/om/när turn whatever follows into a bisats (BIFF word order applies), like clipping a smaller train car onto the main train — it can no longer stand alone as its own sentence.

## 📜 Background — subordinators are built, not invented from scratch

**Att** here plays the same grammatical role as English **that** in "I know **that** she is coming" — both are called complementizers, and both descend from the same family of old demonstrative/pronoun words repurposed to introduce a clause. **Eftersom** is even more transparent about its origin: it's a fossilized compound of **efter** (after) + **som** (as/that, see [03_relativa_pronomen.md](03_relativa_pronomen.md)), literally "after-that" → "because". This is a very common way new subordinating conjunctions are born across languages — combining a simple preposition with a relative particle until the combination calcifies into a single new word with a specialized meaning.

## Examples

- Jag vet **att** hon kommer. (I know that she is coming.)
- Vi stannar hemma **eftersom** det regnar. (We stay home because it's raining.)
- **Om** du vill, kan vi gå. (If you want, we can go.)
- Hon log **när** hon såg mig. (She smiled when she saw me.)
