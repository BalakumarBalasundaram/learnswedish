# Infinitivfraser: att + verb

> 🧠 **Mental model — the USB plug:** "att" is the hyphen connecting two actions into one thought ("Jag vill **att** du kommer"). The plug (att) never changes shape, only the device (the infinitive verb after it) does — like plugging different USB devices into the same port.

## 📜 Background — from "toward" to grammatical glue

Swedish **att**, English **to**, and German **zu** all trace back to the same Proto-Germanic preposition meaning roughly "toward/at". The metaphor is: an infinitive marks a goal you're moving *toward* ("I want [to go]" = my wanting is directed toward the goal of going). Over time this spatial/directional preposition got **grammaticalized** — worn down from a concrete "toward" into an abstract grammatical marker that does nothing but glue a second verb onto the first. This toward-marker-for-infinitives pattern is so common across languages (French **à/de**, German **zu**) that it's considered one of the most natural grammaticalization paths in the world.

## Examples

- Jag vill **att du kommer**. (I want you to come.)
- Hon glömde **att stänga** dörren. (She forgot to close the door.)
- Vi hoppas **att kunna resa** snart. (We hope to be able to travel soon.)
- Det är viktigt **att öva** varje dag. (It's important to practice every day.)
