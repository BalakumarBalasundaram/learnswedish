# Pluskvamperfekt (hade + supinum)

> 🧠 **Mental model — the past-before-the-past:** if preteritum is "yesterday", pluskvamperfekt (hade + supinum) is "the day before yesterday, seen from yesterday" — a flashback inside a story that is itself already in preteritum.

## 📜 Background — stacking one tense trick on top of another

Pluskvamperfekt isn't a separate invention — it's the exact same "have + supinum" trick from [02_perfekt_vs_preteritum.md](02_perfekt_vs_preteritum.md), just with the auxiliary itself pushed one step further into the past: **har → hade** (preteritum of "ha"). Once a language grammaticalizes "have" as a tense-building tool, it's a small, natural next step to conjugate that auxiliary in the past tense too, producing a "past of the past". English does the identical trick: *have eaten → had eaten*. It's a good example of how new grammar rarely appears from nothing — it's usually an existing tool (perfekt) reused one layer deeper.

## Examples

- När jag kom hem, **hade** hon redan **lagat** mat. (When I got home, she had already cooked food.)
- Han **hade redan gått** när jag ringde. (He had already left when I called.)
- Vi **hade** inte **sett** filmen förut, så vi blev förvånade. (We hadn't seen the movie before, so we were surprised.)
