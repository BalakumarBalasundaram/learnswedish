# Partikelverb (Particle Verbs)

> 🧠 **Mental model — docking modules:** a partikelverb is a verb + a tiny "direction arrow" (upp, ut, in, med…) that completely changes its meaning — like a spaceship docking with different modules. ta (take) + upp (up) = "ta upp" (bring up a topic), a totally different meaning from plain "ta". Always keep verb + particle glued as one unit.

## 📜 Background — cousins of English phrasal verbs

Particle verbs aren't a Swedish quirk — they're a **pan-Germanic** feature. English "give up", "look after", "turn on" are built exactly the same way: verb + spatial particle = new, often unpredictable meaning. All of these descend from a common Proto-Germanic system of directional particles that could combine freely with almost any verb. German went one step further and fused many of these particles directly onto the verb as prefixes (**auf-machen**, "open"), while Swedish (like English) kept the particle as a separate, movable word — which is exactly why word order rules for partikelverb (particle before/after the object) matter so much in Swedish grammar.

## Examples

- **ta upp** (bring up) — Vi måste **ta upp** frågan på mötet. (We must bring up the issue at the meeting.)
- **gå ut** (go out) — Ska vi **gå ut** ikväll? (Shall we go out tonight?)
- **komma in** (come in) — Kom **in**! (Come in!)
- **skriva ner** (write down) — Skriv **ner** numret. (Write down the number.)
