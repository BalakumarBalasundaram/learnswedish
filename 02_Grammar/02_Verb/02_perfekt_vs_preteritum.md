# Perfekt vs preteritum

> 🧠 **Mental model — photo vs. receipt:** preteritum is a *photo* of a finished moment with a named past time ("igår åt jag"). Perfekt (har + supinum) is a *receipt* you're still holding now for something that happened at an unspecified time ("jag har ätit"). Named time → preteritum. Still relevant now → perfekt.

## 📜 Background — a construction built out of "have"

Preteritum (åt, bodde, såg) is the old, original simple-past system, inherited straight from Proto-Germanic. **Perfekt** (har + supinum) is much younger — a periphrasis built by grammaticalizing the verb "ha" (to have) as an auxiliary, the same innovation that gave English *"I have eaten"* and French *"j'ai mangé"*. This "have-perfect" spread across much of Europe roughly 1,000–1,500 years ago as neighboring languages influenced each other (a shared areal feature, not just inherited family resemblance). That's why Swedish now needs *two* past systems doing overlapping jobs — the ancient one (preteritum) and the newer, imported-pattern one (perfekt) — layered on top of each other.

## Examples

- Preteritum: Jag **åt** frukost igår. (I ate breakfast yesterday.)
- Perfekt: Jag **har ätit** frukost redan. (I have already eaten breakfast.)
- Preteritum: Hon **bodde** i Stockholm i fem år. (She lived in Stockholm for five years.)
- Perfekt: Hon **har bott** i Stockholm länge. (She has lived in Stockholm for a long time.)
- Preteritum: Vi **såg** filmen förra veckan. (We saw the movie last week.)
- Perfekt: Har du **sett** filmen? (Have you seen the movie?)
