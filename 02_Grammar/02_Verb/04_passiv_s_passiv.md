# Passiv: s-passiv

> 🧠 **Mental model — the silent stamp:** s-passiv just tacks an "-s" onto the verb like a rubber stamp, hiding who did the action ("Boken läses" = the book gets-read, by nobody named). Best for rules, routines, signs, and formal/written Swedish.

## 📜 Background — a fossilized reflexive pronoun

That final **-s** isn't a random passive marker invented from scratch — it's a fossil of the Old Norse reflexive pronoun **"sik"** ("sig" today, see [10_reflexiva_verb.md](10_reflexiva_verb.md)), which fused onto verb endings over time to form what linguists call a **mediopassive**. "Boken läs-s" originally worked like "the book reads itself" — the reflexive meaning slowly bleached into a general passive meaning. This single-suffix passive is a distinctly **North Germanic** feature (Swedish, Norwegian, Danish, Icelandic all have it); English and German never developed it and must always use a separate auxiliary verb ("is read", "wird gelesen") instead.

## 🗂️ Why s-passiv dominates 4 real-world contexts

> 💡 **The common thread:** in all four contexts below, naming *who* does the action would either be obvious, irrelevant, or would make the text sound less objective/official. S-passiv lets Swedish drop the agent entirely (no "man", no "vi", no named subject) — the rule/routine/sign/document just states *what happens*, as if it were a fact of nature rather than someone's personal instruction.

### 1. Regler (Rules) — the rule matters, not the enforcer
A rule is meant to apply to *everyone*, regardless of who is currently checking it — so Swedish rules almost never say "we don't allow X" or "you may not X". They state the rule itself as a standing fact:
- Cyklar **får** inte **parkeras** här. (Bicycles may not be parked here.)
- Mobiltelefoner **ska stängas av** under lektionen. (Mobile phones must be turned off during class.)
- Alkohol **säljs** inte till minderåriga. (Alcohol is not sold to minors.)
- Husdjur **tillåts** inte i matsalen. (Pets are not allowed in the dining room.)
- Betalning **görs** i förskott. (Payment is made in advance.)

> ⚠️ **Curveball:** compare "**Man** får inte parkera här" (informal, still names an implied "one/you") with "Parkering **är förbjuden**" or "Cyklar **får** inte **parkeras** här" (s-passiv/adjective-passive, fully agentless). Official signage and written regulations almost always prefer the fully agentless version — it reads as a neutral fact, not as someone personally forbidding you.

### 2. Rutiner (Routines) — recurring, institutional actions
When something happens the same way every day/week at a workplace, school, or institution, *who* performs it is usually a rotating cast (whichever staff member is on shift) — irrelevant to the routine itself. S-passiv describes the routine as a fixed schedule:
- Frukost **serveras** klockan sju. (Breakfast is served at seven.)
- Lokalerna **städas** varje morgon. (The premises are cleaned every morning.)
- Posten **delas ut** före klockan tolv. (Mail is delivered before twelve.)
- Sopor **hämtas** på tisdagar. (Trash is collected on Tuesdays.)
- Läxor **rättas** samma vecka som de lämnas in. (Homework is corrected the same week it's handed in.)

> 💡 Notice the difference from the *bli*-passiv: a routine is a **repeated, expected process** ("sopor hämtas på tisdagar" — every Tuesday, no exceptions), while bli-passiv usually marks a **single change of state** for one specific subject ("Han blev trött" — see [05_passiv_bli_passiv.md](05_passiv_bli_passiv.md)).

### 3. Skyltar (Signs) — short, impersonal, and (deliberately) not a command
English signage tends to give direct commands ("No smoking", "Keep out", "Do not enter"). Swedish signage very often prefers the softer, impersonal s-passiv instead — it states a fact about the space rather than addressing "you" directly:
- Rökning **är förbjuden** här. (Smoking is forbidden here.)
- Ingången **spärras** klockan 22. (The entrance is closed off at 10 pm.)
- Dörren **hålls** stängd. (The door is kept closed.)
- Detta område **övervakas** med kamera. (This area is monitored by camera.)
- Fotografering **tillåts** inte inne i museet. (Photography is not allowed inside the museum.)

> ⚠️ **Curveball — why this feels "less bossy":** a direct imperative ("Rök inte!" — Don't smoke!) singles out *you*, the reader, as the one being corrected. S-passiv ("Rökning är förbjuden") describes a rule about the *place*, not a command aimed at *you personally* — many learners notice Swedish public signage feels calmer/less confrontational than English signage for exactly this grammatical reason.

### 4. Formellt/skriftspråk (Formal & written registers — myndigheter, avtal, akademiska texter)
Authorities, contracts, and academic writing avoid naming "vi" (we, the agency) or "man" (one) because it would sound personal/subjective, or because the actual person handling each case genuinely varies. S-passiv keeps the tone neutral, procedural, and authoritative:
- Ansökan **behandlas** inom fyra veckor. (The application is processed within four weeks.)
- Beslutet **fattas** av nämnden. (The decision is made by the board.)
- Resultaten **presenteras** i tabell 3. (The results are presented in table 3.)
- Avtalet **undertecknas** av båda parter. (The contract is signed by both parties.)
- Klagomål **hanteras** enligt gällande rutiner. (Complaints are handled according to current procedures.)

> 💡 **Register shift table — same meaning, different formality:**

| Vardagligt (aktiv + man/vi) | Formellt (s-passiv) | Översättning |
|---|---|---|
| **Man** stänger dörren klockan nio. | Dörren **stängs** klockan nio. | The door is/gets closed at nine. |
| **Vi** behandlar din ansökan snart. | Din ansökan **behandlas** snart. | Your application is processed soon. |
| **Vi** skickar paketet imorgon. | Paketet **skickas** imorgon. | The package is sent tomorrow. |
| **Man** får inte röka här. | Rökning **är förbjuden** här. | Smoking is forbidden here. |

> 🎯 **Quick self-test — spot the right register:** which version belongs on an official government letter, and which in a text message to a friend?
> 1. "Vi kollar din ansökan imorgon" vs. "Ansökan **granskas** imorgon." → the s-passiv version is the government-letter register.
> 2. "Jag stänger butiken klockan sex" vs. "Butiken **stängs** klockan sex." → the s-passiv version belongs on the shop's official opening-hours sign.

## Examples

- Boken **läses** av många. (The book is read by many.)
- Dörren **stängs** klockan nio. (The door is/gets closed at nine.)
- Svenska **talas** i Sverige och Finland. (Swedish is spoken in Sweden and Finland.)
- Middagen **lagas** varje kväll. (Dinner is made every evening.)
