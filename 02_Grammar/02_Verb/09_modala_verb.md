# Modala verb: kan, vill, måste

> 🧠 **Mental model — colored lenses:** modal verbs are lenses that color the main verb without ever taking an -r/-er ending themselves: kan (ability lens), vill (desire lens), måste (obligation lens), får (permission lens), ska (plan lens). The real verb behind the lens always stays in bare infinitive (kan **springa**, vill **läsa**).

## 📜 Background — why modals refuse to follow the normal rules

Modal verbs look irregular because they historically *are* a completely different verb class: linguists call them **preterite-present verbs**. Thousands of years ago, their present-tense form was actually an old *past-tense* (ablaut) form that got reinterpreted as a present meaning — which is exactly why **kan** doesn't take an -r ending like a normal Grupp verb would. English shows the identical fossil: **can/could**, **shall/should**, **may/might**, **must** all skip the usual "-s" present-tense ending ("he can", never "he cans") for the same historical reason. It's one of the oldest, smallest, most irregular verb classes in the entire Germanic family — and every Germanic language still has it.

## The 6 modal verbs, conjugated

| Modal | Meaning | Presens | Preteritum | Perfekt (har ...) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| kunna | ability, possibility | kan | kunde | har kunnat |
| vilja | desire, wish | vill | ville | har velat |
| måste* | obligation, necessity | måste | var tvungen att | har varit tvungen att |
| få | permission | får | fick | har fått |
| ska(ll) | plan, future, order | ska | skulle | (rarely used in perfekt) |
| böra | recommendation | bör | borde | har bort |

> \* **måste** has no separate preteritum/perfekt form of its own — Swedish borrows "var/har varit tvungen att" (was/have been forced to) to fill the gap.

## One unbreakable rule: modal + bare infinitive

A modal verb is **always** followed by the main verb in the **bare infinitive** (no "att", no ending):

- ✅ Jag **vill läsa**. (I want to read.)
- ❌ ~~Jag vill att läsa.~~ / ~~Jag vill läser.~~

This holds in every clause type:

| Clause | Word order | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Huvudsats | Subj – modal – (inte) – infinitiv | Jag **kan** **inte** **komma**. |
| Bisats | (att) – subj – inte – modal – infinitiv | ...att jag **inte** **kan** **komma**. |
| Question | Modal – subj – infinitiv | **Kan** du **komma**? |

Negation (**inte**) always sits directly after the modal in a huvudsats, and directly before the modal in a bisats — never attached to the infinitive.

## Examples

- Jag **kan** simma. (I can swim.)
- Hon **vill** åka till Sverige. (She wants to go to Sweden.)
- Vi **måste** gå nu. (We must go now.)
- Ni **får** stanna här. (You may stay here.)
- Han **ska** börja jobba imorgon. (He is going to start work tomorrow.)

## vill vs. ska — desire vs. plan

**vill** expresses a wish/desire (what you *want*), while **ska** expresses a plan, intention, or scheduled future (what you're *going to do*). They are not interchangeable:

- Jag **vill** resa till Japan en dag. (I want to travel to Japan someday. — a wish, no fixed plan)
- Jag **ska** resa till Japan i juni. (I am going to travel to Japan in June. — a fixed plan)
- Han **vill** sluta röka, men han **ska** inte sluta förrän nästa år. (He wants to quit smoking, but he isn't going to quit until next year.)

## The confusing pairs — a side-by-side cheat sheet

| Pair | Difference | Example 1 | Example 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **kan** vs **får** | kan = ability ("am able to") · får = permission ("am allowed to") | Jag **kan** simma. (I know how to swim.) | Jag **får** simma här. (I'm allowed to swim here.) |
| **måste** vs **behöver** | måste = obligation ("must") · **behöver inte** = lack of necessity ("don't need to") — **NOT** "måste inte"! | Du **måste** gå nu. (You must go now.) | Du **behöver inte** gå nu. (You don't need to go now.) |
| **måste inte** vs **behöver inte** | **måste inte** = "must NOT" (forbidden) · **behöver inte** = "don't have to" (optional) | Du **måste inte** röka här. (You must not smoke here — it's forbidden.) | Du **behöver inte** röka om du inte vill. (You don't have to smoke if you don't want to.) |
| **ska** vs **kommer att** | ska = a decided plan/intention · kommer att = a prediction/natural future, no decision involved | Jag **ska** ringa dig ikväll. (I'm going to call you tonight — I've decided.) | Det **kommer att** regna imorgon. (It will rain tomorrow — a prediction.) |
| **vill** vs **skulle vilja** | vill = plain/blunt desire · skulle vilja = polite, softened request | Jag **vill** ha en kaffe. (I want a coffee. — direct) | Jag **skulle vilja** ha en kaffe. (I would like a coffee. — polite) |
| **kan** vs **skulle kunna** | kan = plain ability/possibility · skulle kunna = polite/hypothetical "could" | **Kan** du hjälpa mig? (Can you help me? — neutral) | **Skulle** du **kunna** hjälpa mig? (Could you help me? — more polite) |

> ⚠️ The single most common learner mistake: saying **"måste inte"** when you mean **"don't have to"**. Remember — **måste inte = forbidden**, **behöver inte = optional**.

## skulle — the conditional/polite modal

**skulle** is technically the preteritum of **ska**, but it's used constantly as a stand-alone conditional/polite marker (like English "would"):

- Jag **skulle** vilja beställa en pizza. (I would like to order a pizza. — polite)
- Om jag hade tid, **skulle** jag resa mer. (If I had time, I would travel more. — conditional)
- **Skulle** du kunna stänga fönstret? (Could/Would you close the window? — polite request)
