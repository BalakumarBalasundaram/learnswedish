# Passiv: bli-passiv

> 🧠 **Mental model — the spotlighted event:** bli-passiv = "blev" + perfekt particip, spotlighting a single *change or event* happening to the subject ("Boken blev läst" = it became read, one specific occasion). Rule of thumb: s-passiv = general/habitual, bli-passiv = one completed event.

## 📜 Background — recruiting a "become" verb to do passive duty

**Bli** originally meant something closer to "remain/stay" (it's the same root as German **bleiben**, "to stay"), and only later narrowed to mean "become". Once a language has a solid "become" verb, it's a very common cross-linguistic move to recruit it as a passive auxiliary for single, completed *changes of state* — English does the same thing informally with "get": *"he got hired"* (bli-passiv flavor) vs. the more static *"he is/was hired"* (closer to s-passiv/vara-passiv flavor). So Swedish ended up with **two** passive strategies for two different jobs precisely because it had two different verbs (the fossilized reflexive **-s**, and the repurposed **bli**) available to grammaticalize.

## Examples

- Boken **blev läst** av tusentals människor. (The book was read by thousands of people.)
- Han **blev tillfrågad** om jobbet. (He was asked about the job.)
- Bilen **blev reparerad** igår. (The car was repaired yesterday.)
- Fönstret **blev krossat** under stormen. (The window was broken during the storm.)
