# Reflexiva verb

> 🧠 **Mental model — the mirror bounce:** a reflexive verb is an action that bounces off a mirror and lands back on the subject: "Han tvättar **sig**" = the washing bounces off "sig" and returns to "han" (himself). If the target of the action is the same person doing it, add sig/mig/dig/oss/er.

## 📜 Background — the same "sig" that built the passive

**Sig** is the exact same reflexive root that shows up in **sin/sitt/sina** (see [03_pronomen_possessiva.md](../01_Ordklasser/03_pronomen_possessiva.md)) — and, fused directly onto a verb ending, it's also the historical source of the **s-passiv** (see [04_passiv_s_passiv.md](04_passiv_s_passiv.md)). In Old Norse, reflexive verbs and the emerging passive were the same phenomenon: "tvättar sig" (washes himself) and "tvättas" (gets washed) both grew from the identical reflexive idea of an action folding back onto its own subject, just frozen at different stages — one kept the pronoun as a separate word, the other fused it permanently into the verb's ending.

## Examples

- Han tvättar **sig**. (He washes himself.)
- Jag känner **mig** trött. (I feel tired.)
- Vi förbereder **oss** för provet. (We are preparing ourselves for the test.)
- Hon skyndar **sig** till skolan. (She hurries to school.)
