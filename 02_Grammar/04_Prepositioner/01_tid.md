# Prepositioner: tid

> 🧠 **Mental model — four viewpoints on a timeline:** "på" points at a specific clock-day (på måndag), "i" wraps around a duration like a container (i somras, i två timmar), "om" looks forward to a repeating/future point (om sommaren), "för…sedan" looks backward from now (för två år sedan). Ask: exact day, contained span, future/repeat, or backward-from-now?

## 📜 Background — borrowing space-words to talk about time

Every one of these words started life as a purely **spatial** preposition (i = inside, på = on top of, see [02_plats.md](02_plats.md)) and only later got extended metaphorically to describe time. This "TIME IS SPACE" metaphor is one of the most universal patterns found across the world's languages — humans consistently reuse spatial vocabulary to talk about the more abstract concept of time, because physical space is something we experience directly, while time is not. English does the exact same borrowing, often with different choices than Swedish (**in** a year, **on** Monday, **at** noon) — which is precisely why time prepositions rarely translate word-for-word between languages: each language grammaticalized its own spatial metaphors independently.

## Examples

- **på** måndag (on Monday), **på** morgonen (in the morning)
- **i** somras (last summer), **i** två timmar (for two hours)
- **om** sommaren (in summer / every summer), **om** en vecka (in a week, future)
- **för** två år **sedan** (two years ago)
