# Prepositioner: orsak och syfte

> 🧠 **Mental model — because vs. for:** some prepositions point backward to a reason (**av**, **på grund av**), while others point forward to a purpose (**för**). Ask: am I explaining why something happened, or what it is meant for?

## 📜 Background — from concrete force to abstract reason

Swedish often uses the same small set of prepositions to express very different layers of meaning. **Av** started as a strongly physical relation — separation, source, or the thing that something is made of — and then widened into cause: *han skakade av rädsla* (he shook from fear). **För** is even more flexible: it can mark benefit, purpose, or intended recipient, so *en bok för Anna* feels like something directed toward Anna, not just physically moving there. English does the same thing with **from**, **by**, and **for**, which is why reason and purpose expressions are easy to over-translate word for word.

## Examples

- **av** rädsla (from fear), **av** misstag (by mistake), **av** trötthet (out of tiredness)
- **på grund av** regnet (because of the rain), **på grund av** förseningar (because of delays)
- **för** Anna (for Anna), **för** att lära mig svenska (to learn Swedish), **för** barnens bästa (for the children's best interests)
- **för** din skull (for your sake), **för** att spara tid (in order to save time)
