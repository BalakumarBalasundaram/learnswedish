# Prepositioner: plats

> 🧠 **Mental model — nested boxes:** "i" = inside a container (i huset), "på" = on top of a surface (på bordet), "vid" = right next to (vid sjön), "hos" = at someone's home/place (hos mig). Ask: inside, on-top, next-to, or at-someone's?

## 📜 Background — a preposition born from a noun

**Hos** has an unusually traceable origin: it comes from the old dative form of the noun **hus** (house) — so "hos mig" literally meant something like "at my house" long before it generalized to mean "at someone's place" in any sense (hos doktorn = at the doctor's, not literally the doctor's house). This is a common grammaticalization path called **noun-to-adposition drift**: a concrete noun in a fixed grammatical case slowly loses its noun-like behavior and calcifies into a preposition. French **chez** (from Latin *casa*, "house") went through the identical process — two unrelated languages independently turning "at [someone's] house" into a general-purpose "at [someone's] place" preposition.

## Examples

- **i** huset (in the house), **i** Sverige (in Sweden)
- **på** bordet (on the table), **på** jobbet (at work)
- **vid** sjön (by the lake), **vid** disken (at the counter)
- **hos** mig (at my place), **hos** doktorn (at the doctor's)
