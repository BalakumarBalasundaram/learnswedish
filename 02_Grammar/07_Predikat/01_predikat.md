# Predikat (Predicate)

> 🧠 **Mental model — the predicate is the whole answer to “what is the subject doing / being / like?”** The verb is only the core of that answer, not the whole thing.

## 📜 Background — why "predicate" is bigger than "verb"

The word **predikat** comes from Latin *praedicatum*, "that which is said/asserted" about the subject. This is an old idea from logic, not just grammar: a statement like "Sokrates är dödlig" (Socrates is mortal) has a subject (Sokrates) and something asserted about it (är dödlig). Grammar borrowed this logical split — subject vs. predicate — long before modern syntax trees existed.

Swedish, like other Germanic languages, historically built two very different predicate types from this same logical need:

- **verbal predicate**: the assertion *is* an action — the verb alone carries the whole meaning (springer, äter, skriver).
- **nominal/copular predicate**: the assertion links the subject to a description via a "helper" verb (**vara**, **bli**, **verka**, **kännas**) — the real information sits in the word *after* the verb, not in the verb itself.

This is why **är** in "Hon är lärare" feels almost empty on its own — historically, copula verbs like vara were grammaticalized purely to carry tense/person while the real content (lärare) sits in the complement. The predicate as a category exists precisely to capture both cases under one umbrella, since from the listener's point of view both answer the same question: "what do we know about the subject now?"

## 1) What is a predicate?

The predicate is the part of the clause that says what the subject is doing, is, or becomes.

Examples:

- Jag **springer**. (The predicate is: springer)
- Jag **är trött**. (The predicate is: är trött)
- Hon **kommer ofta**. (The predicate is: kommer ofta)
- Vi **har varit hemma**. (The predicate is: har varit hemma)

The subject is the one who does or is something. The predicate tells us what that is.

## 2) Verb vs predicate

This is the main source of confusion.

### Verb
The verb is the main word of the predicate.

- springer
- är
- kommer
- har varit

### Predicate
The predicate is the whole chunk around the verb.

- springer
- är trött
- kommer ofta
- har varit hemma

So:

- verb = core word
- predicate = full statement about the subject

## 3) Why it is complex

Predicate is tricky because it can be built in different ways.

### A. Verbal predicate (action)

- Jag **springer**.
- Han **äter middag**.
- Vi **skriver ett brev**.

Here the predicate is built around a verb and often includes objects, adverbs, or phrases.

### B. Copular predicate (state/being)

- Jag **är trött**.
- Hon **är sjuk**.
- De **är hemma**.

Here the verb is often a linking verb like **vara**, **bli**, **få**, **kännas**.

The important thing is that the predicate is not just the verb — it includes the description after it.

### C. Predicate with adverb inside it

- Jag **springer snabbt**.
- Jag **är ganska trött**.
- Hon **kommer kanske**.
- Vi **är nästan klara**.

This is exactly why adverbs can feel confusing: they often live inside the predicate even if they are not the verb itself.

## 4) Why “Jag är ganska trött” is a good example

This sentence has:

- subject: Jag
- predicate: är ganska trött
- verb: är
- adjective: trött
- adverb: ganska

The sentence is not describing an action, but a state. Yet the predicate still contains an adverb.

This is why you should not define adverbs only as “words that modify verbs.”

A better definition is:

- adverbs modify the predicate, or parts of it
- they can modify a verb, an adjective, another adverb, or the whole state

## 5) Predicate in Swedish grammar

In Swedish, the finite verb often sits in the middle of the clause, but the predicate can include more than that.

Examples:

- Jag **har inte sett filmen**.
- Hon **kommer säkert imorgon**.
- Vi **har alltid varit hemma**.

In all of these, the predicate is larger than the verb alone.

## 6) A very practical rule

Ask: “What does the subject do / is / become / feel / seem?”

That whole answer is the predicate.

Examples:

- Jag studerar svenska. → predicate = studerar svenska
- Jag är glad. → predicate = är glad
- Jag är väldigt glad. → predicate = är väldigt glad
- Hon kommer snart. → predicate = kommer snart

## 7) Important distinction

The following are not the same:

- verb = the core grammatical word
- predicate = the complete statement about the subject
- adverb = a modifier inside or around that statement

This is exactly why grammar becomes difficult when we move from simple action sentences into state sentences, passive forms, and abstract expressions.

## 8) Compound predicate (sammansatt predikat)

A predicate can contain more than one verb form. This is very common with modal verbs, perfect tense, and infinitive phrases.

- Jag **vill sova**. (I want to sleep.) — modal + infinitive
- Hon **ska resa** imorgon. (She will travel tomorrow.) — modal + infinitive
- Vi **har redan ätit**. (We have already eaten.) — auxiliary + supine
- De **kommer att flytta**. (They are going to move.) — auxiliary + infinitive
- Han **hade kunnat komma**. (He could have come.) — three verb forms in one predicate

The finite verb (the one that carries tense) is always the first verb in the chain. Everything after it — infinitives, supines, particles, objects, adverbs — is still part of the same predicate.

## 9) Predicate complement (predikatsfyllnad)

Some predicates do not describe an action at all — they link the subject (or an object) to a description. This linking part is called **predikatsfyllnad** (predicate complement).

### A. Subject predicative (fyllnad till subjektet)

The complement describes the **subject**, usually with **vara**, **bli**, **verka**, **kännas**, **heta**.

- Hon **är lärare**. (She is a teacher.)
- Han **blev arg**. (He became angry.)
- Det **verkar konstigt**. (It seems strange.)
- Katten **heter Molli**. (The cat is called Molli.)

### B. Object predicative (fyllnad till objektet)

The complement describes the **object**, not the subject.

- Vi målade huset **rött**. (We painted the house red.) — rött describes huset, not vi
- De kallade honom **chefen**. (They called him "the boss".)
- Hon gjorde mig **glad**. (She made me happy.)

This distinction matters because the complement always agrees in gender/number with whatever it describes (subject or object), not with the speaker.

## 10) Predicate and word order (the position schema)

Swedish main clauses follow a fixed slot order that grammarians often draw as a table (the "positionsschema"). A simplified version:

| Fundament | Finite verb | Subject | Sentence adverbial (satsadverbial) | Main verb / rest of predicate | Object | Other adverbials |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Idag | har | jag | inte | sett | filmen | än. |
| Hon | kommer | — | kanske | — | — | imorgon. |
| Vi | ska | — | alltid | äta | middag | tillsammans. |

Two things fall out of this schema:

- the finite verb always sits in the second slot in a statement (the V2 rule)
- sentence adverbials like **inte**, **kanske**, **alltid**, **redan** slot in right after the subject, *before* the rest of the predicate

This is why "Jag har inte sett filmen" feels natural, but "Jag har sett inte filmen" does not — **inte** belongs in the satsadverbial slot, not glued to the object.

## 11) Common mistakes with predicate

- ❌ Treating the predicate as only the verb. Fix: the predicate includes objects, complements, and adverbs — everything that answers "what does/is the subject."
- ❌ Forgetting the complement can describe the **object**, not the subject: "Vi målade huset rött" — rött agrees with huset (ett-word), not with vi.
- ❌ Putting **inte** at the end of the clause out of habit from English ("I don't like it" → "Jag gillar det inte" is actually correct here for objects, but with a full verb phrase it moves: "Jag har inte sett det", not "Jag har sett det inte").
- ❌ Assuming a state sentence ("Jag är trött") has no "real" predicate because there's no action — it does; **är trött** is the predicate.

## 12) Mini test

Identify the predicate (and, where relevant, the complement) in each sentence:

1. Jag springer snabbt.
2. Hon är trött.
3. Vi har redan ätit.
4. De kommer kanske ikväll.
5. Barnen är nästan klara.
6. Vi målade dörren blå.
7. Han vill sova nu.
8. Det verkar svårt.

Answers:

1. springer snabbt
2. är trött
3. har redan ätit
4. kommer kanske ikväll
5. är nästan klara
6. målade dörren blå (blå = object predicative, describes dörren)
7. vill sova nu
8. verkar svårt (svårt = subject predicative, describes det)

## 13) Discussion & practice ideas

- Take five random sentences from a Swedish news article and underline the full predicate, not just the verb.
- For each predicate you find, ask: is there a complement? Does it describe the subject or the object?
- Try building your own compound predicates: modal + infinitive ("vill lära mig"), auxiliary + supine ("har lärt mig"), auxiliary + infinitive ("kommer att lära mig").
- Practice moving **inte** and **kanske** into the correct satsadverbial slot in both main clauses and questions.

## 15) Summary

Predikat is one of the most important concepts in Swedish grammar, but it is also one of the most confusing:

- it is more than the verb — it can be a whole chain of verbs (compound predicate)
- it can include adjectives, adverbs, objects, and complements (predikatsfyllnad)
- it covers both actions and states, and the complement can describe either the subject or the object
- it has a fixed slot in Swedish word order, which is why sentence adverbials like inte/kanske/alltid sit in a very specific place
- it explains why grammar is not only about single words, but about the whole sentence meaning

That is also why adverb feels “big”: because adverbs often live inside the predicate, not only beside the verb.
