> 🧠 **Mental model — stranger vs. old friend:** en/ett introduces a *stranger* walking in for the first time ("en bil" — a car, never seen before). den/det + bestämd form is the *old-friend callback* — you already met it, so you use its nickname ("bilen" — the car).

## 📜 Background — how "the" became a suffix instead of a word

Most languages (English included) put "the" as a separate word *before* the noun. Old Norse did this too, using a demonstrative word `hinn/hin/hitt`. But over centuries that little word drifted backward and **fused onto the end of the noun** — `hinn` glued onto `bátr` (boat) eventually became `báturinn` (the boat). That fossilized fusion is exactly why Swedish marks "the" with a *suffix* (**bil → bilen**) instead of a separate word — it's a demonstrative pronoun that got absorbed into the noun itself, over roughly a thousand years.

Ett litet ord före ett substantiv som visar om något är bestämt eller obestämt. - A small word before a noun that shows definiteness or indefiniteness.

Exanples:
- **En** bil (obestämd) - A car (indefinite)
- **Den** bilen (bestämd) - The car (definite)
- **Ett** hus (obestämd) - A house (indefinite)
- **Det** huset (bestämd) - The house (definite)
- **En** bok (obestämd) - A book (indefinite)
- **Den** boken (bestämd) - The book (definite)
- **Ett** äpple (obestämd) - An apple (indefinite)
- **Det** äpplet (bestämd) - The apple (definite)
- **En** stol (obestämd) - A chair (indefinite)
- **Den** stolen (bestämd) - The chair (definite)
