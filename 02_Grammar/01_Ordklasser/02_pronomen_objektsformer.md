# Pronomen: objektsformer

> 🧠 **Mental model — the receiving end:** subject forms *throw* the action (jag, du, han…), object forms *catch* it (mig, dig, honom…). Rule of thumb: any pronoun standing right after a verb or a preposition must switch to its "catching" form.

## 📜 Background — a fossil of the old case system

Old Germanic languages had four grammatical cases (nominative, accusative, dative, genitive) — a different pronoun shape for every grammatical role. Swedish nouns lost this completely, but pronouns preserve a **merged accusative/dative** as today's "object form": **honom** (him) once separated "I see him" (accusative) from "I gave [to] him" (dative) — modern Swedish uses `honom` for both. That's also why `dem` (them) still shows up in careful/written Swedish even though spoken Swedish increasingly just says `dom` for everything — the object form is slowly eroding, the same slow drift that turned Old English `hine`/`him` into a single modern `him`.

## 🎯 Where object forms show up — a slot map

Object form is a **position rule**, not a meaning rule — it triggers automatically in three slots of a sentence:

```
   SUBJEKT      VERB          OBJEKT-SLOT              PREPOSITION + OBJEKT-SLOT
   ┌──────┐   ┌──────┐   ┌───────────────────┐      ┌──────────────────────────┐
   │  Jag  │──▶│  ser  │──▶│   honom / henne    │      │  med / till / för + dem   │
   └──────┘   └──────┘   └───────────────────┘      └──────────────────────────┘
     (subject form            (object form: direct        (object form: after
      never changes)           or indirect object)          ANY preposition)
```

> 💡 **One test, three triggers:** if the pronoun comes right after a **verb** (Jag ser **honom**), right after **another object** (Hon gav mig **den**), or right after **any preposition** (med **dem**, till **er**, för **oss**) — it must be the object form. The only slot that keeps the subject form is the very front of the clause, before the verb.

## Object forms of personal pronouns
| Subject form | Object form |
|--------------|-------------|
| jag          | mig         |
| du           | dig         |
| han          | honom       |
| hon          | henne       |
| den          | den         |
| det          | det         |
| vi           | oss         |
| ni           | er          |
| de           | dem         |

## Usage rules
- The object form is used when the pronoun is the direct or indirect object of a verb.
- It is also used after prepositions.
- In some cases, the object form can be used for emphasis or contrast.

> ⚠️ **Curveball — "Det är jag" vs. "Det är mig":** grammar books say the pronoun after **är** (is/am/are) should be the *subject* form ("Det är **jag**" — it is I), because `är` doesn't take an object, it just links two equal things. But everyday spoken Swedish overwhelmingly prefers the object form anyway: **"Det är mig"** ("It's me"). This isn't sloppy Swedish — English does the *exact same* thing (formal "It is I" vs. everyday "It's me"), and French does too ("c'est **moi**", not "c'est je"). Across Europe, object forms tend to feel more natural in short, stand-alone answers, even where strict grammar says otherwise.

## Examples
1. Jag såg **honom** igår. (I saw him yesterday.)
2. Hon gav **mig** en bok. (She gave me a book.)
3. Vi pratade med **dem**. (We talked to them.)
4. Kan du hjälpa **oss**? (Can you help us?)
5. De gick till affären utan **er**. (They went to the store without you.)
6. Jag vill inte gå med **dig**. (I don't want to go with you.)
7. Hon älskar **honom**. (She loves him.)
8. Vi såg **den** i parken. (We saw it in the park.)
9. De gav **det** till mig. (They gave it to me.)
10. Jag pratade med **honom** om det. (I talked to him about it.)
11. Hon berättade för **oss** en hemlighet. (She told us a secret.)
12. De frågade **er** om hjälp. (They asked you for help.)
13. Jag såg **dem** på bio. (I saw them at the movies.)
14. Hon gav **mig** en present. (She gave me a present.)
15. Vi pratade med **honom** om resan. (We talked to him about the trip.)

## 🎯 Quick self-test — subject form or object form?

Fill in the correct form of the pronoun shown in parentheses, using the slot map above (right after a verb, another object, or any preposition → object form; front of the clause → subject form):

1. ___ (han) ringde ___ (jag) igår. → _He called me yesterday._
2. Kan ___ (ni) hjälpa ___ (vi)? → _Can you help us?_
3. ___ (de) bor nära ___ (hon). → _They live near her._
4. Ge boken till ___ (jag), inte till ___ (han). → _Give the book to me, not to him._
5. ___ (vi) såg ___ (de) på stationen. → _We saw them at the station._

> 💡 Answers, left to right: **Han / mig · ni / oss · De / henne · mig / honom · Vi / dem**
