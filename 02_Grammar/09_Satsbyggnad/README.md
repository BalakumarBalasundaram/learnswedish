# Satsbyggnad: grammatikmatris

> 🧠 **Mental model — build the sentence from the inside out:** first decide the message, then the verb frame, then the extra pieces around it, and finally the word order that makes the whole sentence sound natural. If you try to think about everything at once, the sentence becomes hard to control; if you build it in layers, the grammar becomes manageable.

## 📜 Background — grammar as a stack, not a list

A real sentence is not chosen by looking at one grammar topic at a time. You first choose the **core meaning**: who or what is involved, what happens, and whether it is a statement, question, or dependent clause. Then you add the supporting layers: adjectives for description, adverbs for time/manner/degree, prepositions for relations, and connectors for linking clauses into a paragraph. Swedish is especially sensitive to this order because **word order** changes meaning and sentence type, while **prepositions** and **pronoun forms** lock in the relationship between words.

## Quick matrix

| Step | Ask yourself | Grammar topics involved | What to choose |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Core message | What is the sentence really about? | Subject, pronouns, nouns, nominal phrase | Pick the main noun or pronoun that carries the topic |
| 2. Action / state | What happens, exists, or changes? | Verb, tense, voice, predicate | Choose the main verb frame and tense |
| 3. Sentence type | Is it a statement, question, or subordinate clause? | Ordföljd, huvudsats, bisats, subjunktioner | Decide the clause pattern before adding details |
| 4. Receiver / target | Who gets the action? | Objekt, indirekt objekt, pronomen objektsformer | Add the object in the right form and order |
| 5. Description | What kind / how many / which one? | Adjektiv, bestämningsord, räkneord | Add agreement, definiteness, and quantity |
| 6. Time / place / direction | When, where, or where to/from? | Prepositioner, tidsadverb, platsadverb, riktning | Attach the right preposition or adverbial |
| 7. Reason / purpose / method | Why, for what, and how? | Prepositioner, adverb, infinitivfraser | Add cause, purpose, instrument, or method |
| 8. Link to other ideas | Does this sentence continue or contrast with another? | Konjunktioner, relativa pronomen, textbindning | Connect the clause to the next one |
| 9. Polish | Does it sound natural and consistent? | Ordföljd, pronoun placement, adjective/adverb choice | Check V2, bisats order, agreement, and focus |

## How to think while writing

Use this order when you build a long sentence or paragraph:

1. Decide the **main clause** first.
2. Fill in the **subject** and **verb**.
3. Add **objects** and **prepositional phrases**.
4. Add **descriptions** with adjectives and adverbs.
5. Add **links** to other clauses if needed.
6. Check the **word order** last.

## Example: one sentence, many grammar layers

**Jag gav min vän en bok om Sverige i går på caféet eftersom han ville lära sig mer.**

| Part | Grammar job |
|---|---|
| Jag | subject pronoun |
| gav | verb in preteritum |
| min vän | indirect object / recipient |
| en bok | direct object with article |
| om Sverige | preposition + topic |
| i går | time adverbial |
| på caféet | place prepositional phrase |
| eftersom han ville lära sig mer | subordinate clause with connector + verb pattern |

## Fast self-check

Before you call a sentence finished, ask:

- Do I know who or what the sentence is about?
- Is the verb form correct for the tense and clause type?
- Are the pronouns in the right form after verbs or prepositions?
- Do the adjectives agree with the noun?
- Are the prepositions expressing the right relation?
- Is the word order natural for a main clause or a bisats?
- Have I linked the clauses clearly?

## Mini reminder

The trick is not to think of grammar as separate islands. Think of it as one pipeline:

**meaning → subject → verb → objects → descriptions → relations → links → word order**
