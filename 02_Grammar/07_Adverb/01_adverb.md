# Adverb (Adverb)

> 🧠 **Mental model — the seasoning:** adverbs never change what the verb *is*, only *how/when/where/how much* it happens — like salt added to a finished dish. Test: if you can slide the word to a different spot in the sentence without breaking the meaning (inte, ofta, snabbt), it's an adverb, not a core ingredient.

## 📜 Background — why so many adverbs look just like neuter adjectives

Notice that `snabb` (adj., common gender) becomes `snabbt` (adj., neuter — see [01_adjektiv_neutrum_plural.md](../06_Adjektiv/01_adjektiv_neutrum_plural.md)) **and** `snabbt` (adverb, "quickly") — same spelling, same -t ending. That's not a coincidence: historically, Swedish (like the other Germanic languages) built most of its adverbs by simply freezing an adjective in its neuter singular form and using it to describe a verb instead of a noun. Once a language has a neuter "default" adjective shape already lying around, it's the path of least resistance to repurpose it as the adverb — no new ending had to be invented.

## Examples

- Hon springer **snabbt**. (She runs quickly.)
- Jag är **inte** trött. (I am not tired.)
- Vi ska **ofta** träffas. (We will meet often.)
- Han pratar **tyst**. (He speaks quietly.)
- De kommer **snart**. (They are coming soon.)
- **Kanske** kommer hon imorgon. (Maybe she'll come tomorrow.)
