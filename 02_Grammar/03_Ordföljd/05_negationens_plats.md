# Negationens plats

> 🧠 **Mental model — "inte" hunts the verb:** in huvudsats "inte" stands right *after* the finite verb ("Jag äter **inte**"); in bisats it jumps to stand *before* the verb ("…att jag **inte** äter"). Same word, opposite side of the verb, depending on clause type.

## 📜 Background — two histories woven together

The huvudsats/bisats flip in negation placement comes from the same source as [02_bisats.md](02_bisats.md)'s BIFF rule — bisats word order is simply older/more conservative, so "inte" sits in its original pre-verb slot there, while huvudsats word order shifted the verb in front of it. Separately, the word **inte** itself has its own history: it descends from Old Norse **"ekki"** ("not a thing"), which itself reinforced an even older, weaker negator (**"ei"**, related to "icke"). This pattern — a negation word wearing thin over time and getting reinforced/replaced by a stronger new word — is called **Jespersen's Cycle**, and it's the exact same process that gave French *"ne…pas"* (pas originally meant "a step") and English *"not"* (originally *"naught", "no whit"*). Negation words are constantly recycled because heavy, frequent use wears down their emphasis over generations.

## Examples

- Huvudsats: Jag äter **inte** köttet. (I don't eat the meat.)
- Bisats: …eftersom jag **inte** äter kött. (…because I don't eat meat.)
- Huvudsats: Hon kommer **inte** imorgon. (She isn't coming tomorrow.)
- Bisats: …att hon **inte** kommer imorgon. (…that she isn't coming tomorrow.)
