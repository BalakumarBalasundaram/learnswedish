# Direkt och indirekt objekt

> 🧠 **Mental model — person before parcel:** the indirect object (the receiver) always stands closer to the verb than the direct object (the thing given): "Hon gav **mig** (indirect) **en bok** (direct)" — hand the person first, then the parcel.

## 📜 Background — a fossil of the old dative case

This "person-before-parcel" ordering isn't arbitrary — it's a direct fossil of the old Germanic **case system**, where the indirect object took **dative** case and the direct object took **accusative** case, and dative-before-accusative was the normal order. Even after Swedish nouns lost their case endings almost entirely, the *word order* that used to signal "this one is the receiver" stuck around as the only remaining clue. English shows the identical fossil: *"She gave me a book"* (indirect-then-direct) works exactly the same way, for exactly the same historical reason — both languages kept the old case-marked order even after the case endings themselves disappeared.

## 🔍 Clues — 4 tests for telling direct from indirect object

Simple concrete sentences ("she gave me a book") are easy — the trouble starts with abstract nouns (ansvar, förtroende, kritik, en förklaring) where nothing is physically "handed over". Run any confusing sentence through these tests, in order:

| # | Test | How it works | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The "vad?" test** | Ask *vad? / what?* about the verb — whatever answers it is the **direct object**, no matter how abstract. | Chefen gav teamet **fullt ansvar**. → Gav vad? → **fullt ansvar** = direct object (even though "ansvar" isn't a physical thing). |
| 2 | **The "till/för"-paraphrase test** | Try rewording the sentence as "...X till/för Y" instead. If it still works, **Y is the indirect object** — this is the single most reliable test for abstract sentences. | Regeringen lovade **medborgarna** en bättre framtid → *Regeringen lovade en bättre framtid **till medborgarna*** ✅ still grammatical → medborgarna = indirect object. |
| 3 | **The passive-promotion test** | In passive voice, only the **direct object** can normally become the new subject. If a noun *can't* become subject of the passive version, it was the indirect object. | Boken **gavs** till mig ✅ (direct object "boken" → subject) · but *Jag gavs boken* sounds odd/marked — mig/jag was never a natural passive subject candidate, confirming it's indirect. |
| 4 | **The "who benefits?" test** | Ask *who received, benefited from, or was affected by* the action (even abstractly) — that's the **indirect object**, regardless of position or form. | Domaren tillerkände **offret** skadestånd. → Who benefited from the ruling? → **offret** (the victim) = indirect object. |

> 💡 **Why abstract sentences feel harder:** with concrete objects (a book, a letter) your intuition about "handing something to someone" already tells you which is which. With abstract nouns (ansvar, kritik, förtroende, en förklaring) there's nothing physical changing hands, so lean on **Test 2 (till/för-paraphrase)** — it works identically whether the direct object is a book or an abstract idea.

## Examples

- Hon gav **mig** (indirekt) **en bok** (direkt). (She gave me a book.)
- Jag skickade **honom** (indirekt) **ett brev** (direkt). (I sent him a letter.)
- Läraren visade **eleverna** (indirekt) **svaret** (direkt). (The teacher showed the students the answer.)

### More abstract & complex examples

- Regeringen lovade **medborgarna** (indirekt) **en bättre framtid** (direkt), trots den ekonomiska krisen. (The government promised the citizens a better future, despite the economic crisis.)
- Chefen gav **teamet** (indirekt) **fullt ansvar** (direkt) för projektet, vilket överraskade alla. (The boss gave the team full responsibility for the project, which surprised everyone.)
- Domaren tillerkände **offret** (indirekt) **ett stort skadestånd** (direkt) efter en rättegång som pågått i flera månader. (The judge awarded the victim significant damages after a trial that had lasted several months.)
- Forskarna erbjöd **allmänheten** (indirekt) **en ny förklaring** (direkt) till fenomenet, även om flera experter fortfarande tvivlade. (The researchers offered the public a new explanation for the phenomenon, even though several experts still doubted it.)
- Styrelsen gav **vd:n** (indirekt) **sitt fulla förtroende** (direkt), samtidigt som aktieägarna krävde förändring. (The board gave the CEO their full confidence, while shareholders demanded change.)
- Läraren gav **eleverna**, **som hade kämpat hela terminen**, (indirekt) **ett uppmuntrande omdöme** (direkt). (The teacher gave the students, who had struggled all term, an encouraging assessment.)
