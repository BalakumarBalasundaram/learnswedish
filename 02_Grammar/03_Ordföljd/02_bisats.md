# Ordföljd: bisats

> 🧠 **Mental model — the quiet room (BIFF):** inside a bisats the verb never moves to seat 2 — subject-verb order stays put. Remember **BIFF**: **B**isats, **I**nte (negation) jumps **before** the verb, **F**ull verb **F**öljer — the opposite of where "inte" goes in a huvudsats.

## 📜 Background — why subordinate clauses play by different rules

The huvudsats/bisats split exists because the V2 rule (see [01_huvudsats.md](01_huvudsats.md)) historically only ever applied to **independent, main clauses** — subordinate clauses were treated as a more conservative, less "innovated" part of the grammar and kept an older word-order pattern. German shows an even more extreme version of this same split: German subordinate clauses push the verb all the way to the **end** of the clause, while Swedish only keeps it from moving to seat 2. Both languages are preserving the same underlying historical fact — subordinate/embedded clauses resisted the V2 innovation that transformed main clauses.

## Examples

- …att jag **inte** äter äpplet. (…that I don't eat the apple.)
- Jag vet att hon **redan** har gått. (I know that she has already left.)
- Han sa att han **aldrig** ljuger. (He said that he never lies.)
